\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{A practical rule.}
Which channel carries a written value is set by the tokens that follow the writing token, not by that token alone. A component-level claim about in-context computation, about a circuit or an intervention, should therefore name the prompt structure under which it was measured, above all whether and where the candidate values are mentioned after the writing token, and should be tested under a format with a later mention and one without. Moving the same candidates before the writing token (\fmtBefore{}, \fmtSentB{}) is a cheap control: it keeps the words, the question and the answer position but removes the readers (\cref{tab:twobytwo}). \Cref{app:discussion} relates our results to address-versus-payload accounts of lookback \citep{prakash2025lookbacks,wu2026record}.

\paragraph{Implications for evaluation and attribution.}
A mechanism localised with the candidates listed after the written value, as in multiple-choice benchmarks \citep{mueller2025mib}, may route it through a different channel than the free-form behaviour it should explain. We infer, but have not tested, that attribution methods that freeze attention patterns would miss the key channel, which QK attributions \citep{kamath2025attention} may capture.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i)~The tasks are synthetic and templated, and every value is a single token. (ii)~Our account was refined post hoc: a ``listed options only'' account formed and confirmed at 1.5B fails from 7B up, and the later-mention account that replaced it was then tested by preregistration (D3, E1). (iii)~A single-channel clamp pairs a source key with a base value, a state no input produces; the joint clamp is exact and $\mathrm{ID}_K$ cancels non-specific effects, but such states may be off-distribution. (iv)~The fall in key share beyond 14B within Qwen2.5 is unexplained; we have ruled out only the software environment. (v)~The small residual key identity under \fmtNone{} and the negative $\mathrm{ID}_K$ under \fmtBefore{} on the belief task (\cref{sec:mentions}) are unexplained. (vi)~The revisit of \citet{anonymous2026fitted} covers one kind of intervention at two models and one question, and the refit, which changed only the fitting prompt, one model.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Across four model families and three tasks, later mentions of the candidates decide whether a written value is looked up through its key or copied from its value; where we localised the read, the re-mentioned words are the readers. A released intervention has nearly the same behavioural effect in every format we tested, yet the channel that appears to carry it moves from keys to values as later mentions are weakened or removed, and at Mistral-Small-24B a refit without later mentions does the same.
