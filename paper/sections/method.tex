\section{Setup}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Stories, runs and formats.}
We use the belief stories of \citet{anonymous2026fitted} (``[\dots] \{agent\} watches as the \{$o$\} is moved to the \{\textsc{loc}\}. \{other\} does not see this happen. [\dots] Where does \{agent\} believe the \{$o$\} is?''; \cref{app:prompts}). Base and source stories B and S differ only at the writing token \{\textsc{loc}\}, at position $p$; a third value X is absent from the story. Prompts end with an ``Answer:'' prefill. \fmtOpt{} lists the candidates after the question (``Answer with exactly one choice''), \fmtLetter{} labels them A--F; under ``Answer with one word'', \fmtListA{} lists them there, \fmtPost{} names them in a neutral sentence after the story, \fmtNull{} replaces it by one of the same length that names none, \fmtSentB{} and \fmtBefore{} put the sentence or the list before the story, and \fmtNone{} names none. A \emph{later mention} is any token after $p$ that names a candidate. The \emph{fresh stories} are 150 new cores with eight new sentences (each with its null version), a second lexicon in half of the cores and per-core candidate orders (\cref{app:fresh}).

\paragraph{Passages.}
From the SQuAD v1.1 development set \citep{rajpurkar2016squad} we take questions whose answer is a person, place or number named once in the passage, and write a counterfactual entity in its place \citep{longpre2021conflicts}: 185 items from 34 articles, with answers of mostly two to four tokens. The writing token is then the answer span, clamped at every position. Formats: options after the question (answered by name or letter) or before the passage, a related-articles sentence after or before it, or free form. We score the first answer token after each model's answer frame, fixed from its own generations before any test; a closed-book prior for the original answer cancels from the identity measures to first order (\cref{app:natural}).

\paragraph{Clamps.}
$\mathcal{C}_K(Z)$ sets the writing token's pre-RoPE key in every layer from $\ell_0$ ($0$ unless stated) to the one cached in run $Z$, and $\mathcal{C}_V(Z)$ its value. Later tokens see the writing token only through these, so clamping both from layer~0 reproduces run $Z$ exactly (checked in FP32 before any model output; \cref{app:impl}). A single-channel clamp creates a cache state that no prompt produces, which is why we also measure generated answers.

\paragraph{Measures.}
With $\Delta_Y(Z)$ the change in $\log p(Y)$ at the answer under $\mathcal{C}_K(Z)$ against a self-clamp row in the same batch,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:idk}
\mathrm{ID}_K = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[\Delta_{\mathrm{S}}(\mathrm{S}) - \Delta_{\mathrm{S}}(\mathrm{X}) + \Delta_{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{X}) - \Delta_{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{S})\big],
\end{equation}
so effects shared by all foreign keys, and a prior for B, cancel. $\mathrm{ID}_V$ is defined likewise with values, $s_{\mathrm{ID}} = \overline{\mathrm{ID}}_K/(\overline{\mathrm{ID}}_K + \overline{\mathrm{ID}}_V)$ is a ratio of means, and $r$ is a format's $\overline{\mathrm{ID}}_K$ relative to that with the list after the story. Under the \emph{emitted-form score}, a candidate's probability is summed over the surface forms the model emits, found from its own generations on held-out stories, and coverage of at least 0.8 is required in every cell; the discovery sample keeps its score on the lower-case word, and every verdict re-scored under emitted forms is unchanged (risky; held; \cref{sec:fresh}). Under each clamp we also decode greedily: $\beta_K$ ($\beta_V$) is the share of answers that move to the clamped value under the key-only (value-only) clamp, net of moves that a clamp to the third value also causes; the flip rate omits that correction.

\paragraph{Models, statistics and preregistration.}
The discovery sample has \nModels{} models: Qwen2.5 at 1.5B--72B \citep{yang2024qwen25}, Qwen3-8B, Mistral-7B, Mistral-Small-24B \citep{mistral2025small} and OLMo-2-7B \citep{olmo2025two}; the final round adds Llama-3.1-8B \citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, Gemma-2-9B and Gemma-2-2B \citep{gemma2024gemma2}, Phi-4 \citep{abdin2024phi4} and Falcon3-7B \citep{tii2024falcon3}. All are instruction-tuned and run in BF16; the models of the final round are official weights (sha256-verified; \cref{app:compute}). Intervals are 95\,\% bootstrap percentile intervals with 10{,}000 resamples, in the final round two-stage over clusters where defined (location pair and lexicon, or article). A prediction over several models must hold in every evaluable model, one over the four new families in three of them (98.75\,\% intervals); equivalence tests for the law of \cref{sec:claim3} use 90\,\% intervals; a Holm correction changes no verdict (\cref{app:prereg-j}). Each of ten preregistrations was committed before its run, with its scoring code fixed before any output was inspected; risky means a recorded probability of holding below 0.8. Unless marked otherwise, a result in \cref{sec:claim1,sec:claim2,sec:claim3} was preregistered in this final round and held. We mark risky predictions, say in words where a prediction did not hold or could not be evaluated, and label results of the discovery sample, exploratory analyses and post hoc readings; \cref{app:crosswalk} maps every sentence to its prediction and verdict.
