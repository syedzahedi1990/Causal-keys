\section{Setup}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Task and prompt formats.}
We generate belief stories with a port of the released generator of \citet{anonymous2026fitted}: ``Everyone initially sees that the \{$o$\} is in the \{init\}. [\dots] \{$A$\} watches as the \{$o$\} is moved to the \{\textsc{loc}\}. \{$B$\} does not see this happen.'', followed by ``Where does \{$A$\} believe the \{$o$\} is?'' (template in \cref{app:prompts}). Locations are six single-token words. A base story B and a source story S differ only at the \emph{writing token} \{\textsc{loc}\} at position $p$. Two further tasks write the value with one sentence: \emph{paint} (``Later, \{agent\} repainted the \{thing\} \{colour\}.'', six colours) and \emph{schedule} (``Later, \{agent\} moved the \{thing\} to \{day\}.'', seven days). Every prompt is wrapped as a system turn, a user turn, the generation prompt with thinking disabled and an ``Answer:'' prefill, and we read full-vocabulary log-probabilities at the answer position. The formats differ in where, if anywhere, the candidates are named and in the answer instruction (\cref{app:prompts}): \fmtOpt{} (options after the question, ``Answer with exactly one choice'', the format of \citealt{anonymous2026fitted}); \fmtLetter{} (options A--F after the question, ``Answer with one letter'', scored on the letters); and, all with ``Answer with one word'', \fmtPost{} (the neutral sentence ``The room has a box, a basket, \dots{} and a closet.'' after the story), \fmtNone{} (no candidates) and \fmtBefore{} (the options list before the story). On the new tasks the neutral sentence is task-specific and the schedule letters run to G. A \emph{later mention} is any token after $p$ that names a candidate. The instruction-matched 2$\times$2 of \cref{sec:mentions} adds \fmtListA{} (\fmtOpt{} with the one-word instruction) and \fmtSentB{} (the sentence before the story).

\paragraph{Clamping the key and value channels.}
A later token sees the writing token only through its cached key, which decides how much the token attends to $p$, and its value, which decides what is copied when it does. We call these the \emph{key channel} and the \emph{value channel}. The clamp $\mathcal{C}_K(Z)$ runs the base prompt but sets, at $p$ and in every layer $\ell \ge \ell_0$, the pre-RoPE \texttt{k\_proj} output (all KV groups) to the one cached from run $Z$; $\mathcal{C}_V(Z)$ does the same for \texttt{v\_proj}, and $\mathcal{C}_{KV}(Z)$ does both. Unless stated otherwise $\ell_0 = 0$, and then $\mathcal{C}_{KV}(\mathrm{S})$ reproduces the source run at every later position.\footnote{Positions before $p$ have identical inputs in B and S and hence identical keys and values; at $p$ both are set to S's in every layer; by induction over layers every later position receives the same inputs as in S. The clamp precedes key normalisation (Qwen3, OLMo-2) and RoPE, which both runs apply identically at $p$. We confirm this numerically (\cref{app:impl}).}

\paragraph{Measures.}
With $m_{\mathrm{SB}} = \log p(\mathrm{S}) - \log p(\mathrm{B})$ at the answer position, the effect of clamping channel $C$ is measured against the \emph{self-clamp row} $\mathcal{C}_{KV}(\mathrm{B})$, computed in the same batch:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dc}
d_C = m_{\mathrm{SB}}\big(\mathcal{C}_C(\mathrm{S})\big) - m_{\mathrm{SB}}\big(\mathcal{C}_{KV}(\mathrm{B})\big),
\end{equation}
for $C \in \{K, V, KV\}$. The key share is $s_K = \bar d_K / (\bar d_K + \bar d_V)$, a ratio of means over stories. A foreign key can move $m_{\mathrm{SB}}$ without carrying the source value, for instance by weakening B. To isolate identity we also clamp from a story with a third value X that is absent from the story, and with $\Delta_Y(Z) = \log p(Y \mid \mathcal{C}_K(Z)) - \log p(Y \mid \mathcal{C}_{KV}(\mathrm{B}))$ we define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:idk}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{ID}_K = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[ &\Delta_{\mathrm{S}}(\mathrm{S}) - \Delta_{\mathrm{S}}(\mathrm{X}) \\
 + &\Delta_{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{X}) - \Delta_{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{S}) \big].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Any effect shared by all foreign keys cancels, so $\mathrm{ID}_K$ (in nats) is zero if keys carry no identity of the value; $\mathrm{ID}_V$ is defined in the same way with values. Because nats grow with the model's confidence, we also report the scale-free \emph{identity key share} $s_{\mathrm{ID}} = \overline{\mathrm{ID}}_K/(\overline{\mathrm{ID}}_K + \overline{\mathrm{ID}}_V)$, which, unlike $s_K$, excludes non-specific effects.
The \emph{row-restricted splice} runs attention twice per layer and splices output rows by a mask, so that only a chosen group of later tokens sees the swapped key; it is exact (in an FP32 unit test, all rows reproduce the full key clamp and no rows the clean run, to within $10^{-4}$ in the logits; \cref{app:impl}).
The \emph{role-swap control} swaps the observer and non-observer names in the event while keeping the location word, which moves the belief answer from \textsc{loc} to the initial location; $f_C$ is the fraction of this change carried by the writing token's channel $C$ alone.

\paragraph{The intervention of Anonymous (2026).}
\citet{anonymous2026fitted} patch Mistral-Small-24B and Qwen2.5-72B at the output of (1-based) decoder block 4 over every token of the event span (from ``\{$A$\} watches as'' to the end of the story). For a rank-16 basis $U$ with orthonormal rows, each span position becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:patch}
h \leftarrow h_{\mathrm{B}} + U^{\top} U\,(h_{\mathrm{S}} - h_{\mathrm{B}}).
\end{equation}
In the learned remap M, $U$ is a distributed alignment search basis \citep{geiger2024finding} fit with a six-choice cross-entropy objective so that the patched model ranks $\mathrm{T} = \pi(\mathrm{S})$ first, where $\pi$ is a fixed pair-swap of the six locations; it was fit without the ``Answer:'' prefill of our formats, which \citet{anonymous2026fitted} add only at evaluation. In the control P, $U$ is an unfitted PCA basis of source-minus-base differences, which transfers S. We reuse the released bases (three seeds each; the Qwen2.5-72B PCA bases are reconstructions, labelled so by \citet{anonymous2026fitted}). Following their design, a \emph{key-only exchange} replaces the writing token's key in every block from (1-based) block 6 on by the key of the other patched run, while queries, values and residual states evolve freely; we add the symmetric \emph{value-only exchange}. Unlike our clamp, these exchanges are not exact, because the patch also changes the other span tokens. With $m_{\mathrm{TS}} = \log p(\mathrm{T}) - \log p(\mathrm{S})$, writing $\mathrm{P}{+}K_{\mathrm{M}}$ for P with M's keys and $m(\cdot)$ for $m_{\mathrm{TS}}$ in a run,
\begin{align}
\varphi &= \frac{m(\mathrm{M}) - m(\mathrm{P})}{m(\mathrm{T}) - m(\mathrm{S})}, \label{eq:phi}\\
\psi_K &= \frac{m(\mathrm{P}{+}K_{\mathrm{M}}) - m(\mathrm{P})}{m(\mathrm{M}) - m(\mathrm{P})}, \label{eq:psik}\\
\psi_V &= \frac{m(\mathrm{P}{+}V_{\mathrm{M}}) - m(\mathrm{P})}{m(\mathrm{M}) - m(\mathrm{P})}. \label{eq:psiv}
\end{align}
$\varphi$ is the shift of M beyond P towards T, as a fraction of the natural S$\to$T shift; $\psi_K$ and $\psi_V$ are the fractions of the learned-minus-PCA difference that M's keys or values add to P. The removal fractions $\rho_K = [m(\mathrm{M}{+}K_{\mathrm{P}}) - m(\mathrm{M})]/[m(\mathrm{P}) - m(\mathrm{M})]$ and $\rho_V$ (the same with $V_{\mathrm{P}}$) give M the keys or values of P instead (\cref{app:impl}). All are ratios of means over the 96 of their 120 native story cores in which B, S and T are distinct, after averaging the three fits within each core. As a preregistered gate, the argmax token of M and of P under \fmtOpt{} must agree with their released outputs on at least 0.95 of the (core, fit) items of each model, over all 120 cores; agreement is 0.994--1.000.

\paragraph{Models, statistics and preregistration.}
We study \nModels{} instruction-tuned models from four families: Qwen2.5-Instruct \citep{yang2024qwen25} at 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B, 32B and 72B, Qwen3-8B, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Mistral-Small-24B-Instruct-2501 \citep{mistral2025small} and OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct \citep{olmo2025two}, in BF16 on GPU. We use $n = 150$ stories per format and model. The row-restricted splice uses $n = 60$ at 7--14B; at Qwen2.5-1.5B it ran, like preregistration A, on CPU in FP32 with $n = 40$. Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from 10{,}000 bootstrap resamples of stories, with ratios recomputed within each resample. Six preregistrations (A--F) were each committed to version control before their run, with the scoring script committed before its outputs were inspected. \Cref{app:prereg} gives every prediction with its recorded outcome, including the failed ones, close to the wording of the log, with format names mapped to ours, and classifies every main claim as confirmatory, exploratory or post hoc.
