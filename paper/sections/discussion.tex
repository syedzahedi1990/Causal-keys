\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{A practical rule, and a hypothesis.}
Which channel carries a written value is set by the tokens after the writing token, so a component-level claim should state where the candidates are mentioned after it, and be tested with and without a later mention; moving them before the writing token (\fmtBefore{}, \fmtSentB{}) is a cheap control that keeps the words but removes the readers (\cref{tab:twobytwo}). A mechanism localised on multiple-choice benchmarks \citep{mueller2025mib} may use another channel than the free-form behaviour it should explain; we infer, but have not tested, that attributions freezing attention patterns miss the key channel, which QK attributions \citep{kamath2025attention} may capture. As an observation, not a finding: the format moved the attributed channel of an identity edit strongly at block~0 ($+\HelevenDiff{}$) and of the binding swap weakly at block~28 ($+\HnineDiff{}$); whether edit kind or depth decides this is untested; it needs several edit kinds at matched depth.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i)~Three synthetic, templated tasks with single-token values; on IOI a later list's key read is not established. (ii)~A post hoc ``listed options only'' account failed from 7B up; its replacement was then preregistered (D3, E1). (iii)~A single-channel clamp may be off-distribution. (iv)~The fall in key share beyond 14B and (v)~the small key identity under \fmtNone{} and its sign under \fmtBefore{} are unexplained. (vi)~The revisit covers two edits from two labs: the remap of \citet{anonymous2026fitted} (two models; refit at one) and the binding swap. (vii)~Heads and hop~2 were tested at two 7B models in BF16, in one format; the readers at 24B and 72B are inferred, and (viii)~a BF16 batch-shape offset at Qwen2.5-7B makes the same-heads claim across formats not evaluable. (ix)~The binding swap was tested at one model (H12 not run) and flips no answer when candidates are named. (x)~At block~0 the identity edit nearly restates the natural clamp (H11). (xi)~No depth has both the binding swap and a key-read identity route (Gate~b3); kind and depth are confounded.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Across four model families and three templated tasks, later mentions of the candidates decide whether a written value's identity is looked up through its key or mainly copied from its value. At two 7B models the lookup has two hops: heads with a duplicate-token core read the key at the re-mentioned words, and the answer reads those words, mainly through their keys (unscored). Identity edits appear to act in the channel the format's readers use; on another lab's binding swap, which is key-dominant, our preregistered extension failed.
