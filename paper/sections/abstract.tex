A token that states a value, such as \emph{shelf} in ``the candle is moved to the shelf'', can reach a later answer in two ways: a later token holding a candidate can match its cached key and look the value up, or the answer can copy its cached value. Clamping this writing token's key and value separately, we find that later mentions decide the route. When the candidates are named again after the token, its key carries which word was written (most of it after a list, part after a neutral sentence); otherwise, as after a mention before the token or a sentence naming no candidate, the identity is copied. In seven model families the route decides generated answers: clamping only the key changes them when the candidates are listed after the story, and clamping only the value does the reverse. On counterfactual reading-comprehension passages an options list after the passage likewise opens a key read in every evaluable model, though we do not show that it decides answers there. In two 7B models the matching heads write, at the written word's later mention, a component that names no candidate and that the answer reads by key; it suffices to move the answer but is neither specific nor the only carrier. A preregistered law that edits keep the model's own key-to-value ratio failed, even for near-natural steering vectors.
