\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Readout-relative explanations.}
A context token reaches later tokens only through its key and its value, and our results show that the readout decides which of the two carries a state written at that token. With the candidates listed after the state token, most of the answer effect travels through the key (key share \shareQwenSeven{}--\shareQwenFourteen{} under \fmtOpt{} in Qwen2.5 at 7--14B). A neutral re-mention sentence opens the key channel only partially, and only from 7B up. With nothing re-mentioned, keys carry at most \noneMax{} nats of identity and the state is copied from the value. The same pattern holds for an intervention whose behavioural effect barely depends on the readout (\cref{tab:frames}). We draw a practical rule from this. A component-level causal claim, about an intervention or about a circuit, should name the readout under which it was measured, and should be tested under at least one alternative readout, preferably one with and one without a post-state re-mention of the candidates. The \fmtBefore{} arm is a cheap control for this: it names the same candidates as \fmtOpt{} but places them before the story, where under the causal mask they cannot attend to the state token, while the question and the answer position still follow it (like \fmtNone{}, it asks for a free-form answer).

\paragraph{What this means for \citet{anonymous2026fitted}.}
Our results sharpen their conclusion rather than overturn it. Their key-exchange result holds qualitatively under their answer format, \fmtOpt{}, measured here as fractions of log-odds rather than as their agreement rates ($\psi = \psiOptQwen{}$, $\rho = \rhoOptQwen{}$ at Qwen2.5-72B; $\psi = \psiOptMistral{}$, $\rho = \rhoOptMistral{}$ at Mistral-24B), and their learned remap changes behaviour by a nearly constant fraction of the natural shift in every readout we tried. What does not survive a change of readout is the attribution: under \fmtNone{} the critical-token keys carry little of the learned--PCA difference (key-only addition at most \psiNoneMistral{}, removal at most \rhoNoneMistral{}), and under \fmtBefore{} they carry none of it. They state their finding as a causal contribution of keys conditional on the patched computation; the readout is a further condition. The remap is written redundantly into both channels, and the multiple-choice readout reads it mainly through the key. The preregistered transfer law adds a caution in the other direction (\cref{sec:res-failed}): it fails at Mistral-24B and holds only at the edge of its margin at Qwen2.5-72B, and in both models the value-channel completeness estimated by clamping under one readout underestimates how fully the value channel carries the remap under another.

\paragraph{Relation to address and payload accounts of binding.}
Lookback and rebinding accounts retrieve bound information by attention: a binding address, expressed in query/key subspaces \citep{oh2026rebinding}, selects which earlier token is read, and the bound payload is then retrieved from that token \citep{prakash2025lookbacks,oh2026rebinding}. Our \fmtNone{} results agree with the payload half: the location is copied from the value. They do not test the address half, because $\mathrm{ID}_K$ measures location-specific content in the key, and an address shared by base and source runs would not register. The re-mention case adds a route that is distinct from lookup by binding address. The state token's key carries the location's identity, and later mentions of the candidates match against it: at Qwen2.5-1.5B the option words recover \locOptChoicewords{} of the key effect under \fmtOpt{}, and an exploratory attention probe, run in an earlier prompt configuration, shows the option word that matches the key's location attending to the state token as it does in the source run. This suggests an identity-matching lookup by content; whether it operates alongside an address-based lookup we do not test. Whether the same readers carry the read at 7--14B, and under \fmtPost{}, is the subject of stage~3. \todo{Stage 3: update with the D3 localisation verdicts at Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-14B and Mistral-7B.}

\paragraph{Implications for evaluation and safety-relevant interpretability.}
Multiple-choice formats are common in interpretability benchmarks, such as the MCQA task of MIB \citep{mueller2025mib}, and in behavioural evaluations. Our results suggest that a mechanism found under such a format can route the same state through a different component than the free-form behaviour it is meant to explain, even when the behaviour itself is unchanged. One consequence, which we infer but have not tested, concerns attribution methods that hold attention patterns fixed, such as attribution graphs, which freeze attention patterns to keep attributions linear \citep{kamath2025attention}. Such methods remove the key channel's influence by construction, so under re-mention readouts they would miss the key share of the effect, which on our task is most of the \fmtOpt{} effect in every model at 7B and above (\cref{tab:natural}). Methods that model query--key interactions explicitly \citep{kamath2025attention} may be better placed to see this route. For mechanistic claims that inform safety decisions, the readout belongs in the claim.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i) All stories are synthetic belief stories with one template; generality to other state-writing tasks is pending \todo{Stage 3: paint and schedule results (D1, D2).} (ii) Every state is a single token, so we do not know how the channels divide when a state spans several tokens. (iii) The ``listed options only'' account was formed post hoc at 1.5B; it passed a same-model fresh-seed test but does not generalise to $\geq$7B, and the account we now give (from 7B up, any post-state re-mention opens the key channel, an options list most strongly; at 1.5--3B only an options list does) was formed after the stage-1 data and was not preregistered, although the three stage-2 models agree with it (\cref{tab:natural}). (iv) The scale curve comes from one family and is non-monotonic beyond 14B; the preregistered threshold $s_K \geq 0.80$ at 24--72B was met only at Qwen2.5-32B, and we have no explanation for the fall at 32B and 72B. (v) Under \fmtNone{}, keys carry a small residual identity of up to \noneMax{} nats in the Qwen models from 7B to 32B, outside the $\pm 1$ nat margin preregistered at 1.5B. (vi) A single-channel clamp combines a source key with a base value, a state that no input produces and that may lie off the model's distribution. The joint clamp reproduces the source run exactly, effects are measured against identity rows in the same batch, and $\mathrm{ID}_K$ cancels non-specific effects of a foreign key, but none of this makes the single-channel states natural. (vii) The mechanisms behind the residual \fmtNone{} effect and the interaction of \fmtPost{} with scale are not established. (viii) The exact localisation has so far been run only at Qwen2.5-1.5B, and the revisit of \citet{anonymous2026fitted} covers one intervention in two models.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The readout decides which attention channel carries in-context state. When the candidates are listed after the state token, the state is read through the state token's key: under \fmtOpt{} the key carries the location's identity in all \nModels{} models from 1.5B to 72B, and most of the answer effect in every model except Qwen2.5-1.5B. Without any re-mention the state is copied from the value, and a neutral re-mention sentence opens the key channel only partially, and only from 7B up. A structural control, options before the story, leaves keys with at most \beforeMax{} nats of identity in any model. The learned remap of \citet{anonymous2026fitted} changes behaviour by a nearly constant fraction of the natural shift under all five readouts we tested, yet its attribution to critical-token keys is large only when the candidates are listed after the state token, partial with a neutral re-mention sentence, and small or absent without one. Component-level explanations of in-context state and of interventions are therefore relative to the readout under which they are measured. We recommend naming that readout in every such claim and testing it under at least one alternative.
