### would_move_score
Scope: Part E only (the framing, novelty argument, related work, abstract and presentation). I assume Parts A-D come out as their own critics' revised versions predict. Scores are on the panel's scale (mechinterp 4, sceptic 4, generalist 4, stats 5).

AS DESIGNED, IF A-D LAND
- Mechinterp: 4 -> 4.5 (presentation 2 -> 3; contribution stays 2).
  - The novelty paragraph can be refuted with this reviewer's own question and the paper's own data. Their Q1 asks for a case 'other than the 1.5B/3B dissociation', and regularity (b) is that dissociation.
  - Regularity (c), 'the question sets the sign', follows from competence on a 'not mentioned' question plus IOI's duplicate-to-S-inhibition path (Wang et al. 2022).
  - Table 1 row 1 says 'no later token reads it', but LIST-BEFORE is negative in 9/10 models (down to -2.0 nats at 72B).
  - The closest prior work they would bring up has been dropped. Wu & Shomali 2026 (KV-cache 'routing record' vs payload) and Ok & Lee 2026 (causal mask hides the context from options placed before it) were both cited in v4.
- Sceptic: 4 -> 4.5. Self-containment, the adopted 'lookup' definition and caveats in the main text are credited. The abstract's overclaims are penalised:
  - 'even in a neutral sentence ... looked up', although s_ID(POST) is 0.19-0.41 at 7B, so the value still carries most of the identity;
  - 'seven families up to 72B ... on fresh stories and passages', although 72B is only in the discovery sample;
  - 'equals';
  - 'small models make the match without using it', which is confounded with competence.
- Generalist: 4 -> 5. The clearer paper and the practice box help. The box rests partly on A7/HA3, which A's critic showed are prior-confounded or near-tautological, and on an exploratory KIVI link. A careful reader discounts it to about 4.5.
- Stats: 5 -> 5 (soundness 3 -> 3.5).
  - This reviewer will see that the risk rule (no data from the same model x format x measure cell) labels extrapolations as risky. Examples are JB1/JB2/JB4 in new families, whose priors are about 0.9 or higher.
  - They will also see that A-I labels are assigned after the outcomes are known and pooled into the abstract's 'N of M risky' figure. That is the uncalibrated tally they criticised, in a new form.
- Panel mean: about +0.4.

WITH THE REQUIRED CHANGES, IF A-D LAND
- Mechinterp: 4 -> 5, and 5.5 if D1-D4 and C's lexical rule land. The changes that earn this:
  - a three-column Table 1 that concedes architecture and known circuits, with citations;
  - novelty restated as three measured, risky results: a later list recruits selection even under a free-answer instruction; there is no second-order lookup after an earlier list; and the key channel reads only the token-form part of a written value, for natural and edited values alike;
  - the closest work positioned precisely.
- Sceptic: 4 -> 5.
- Generalist: 4 -> 5.5, driven by the cue-conflict sentence: 'the same cache gives the key's entity in MCQ and the value's entity in free form'.
- Stats: 5 -> 6 (soundness 4), from:
  - prior-based tiers, set before the run and unified across A-D;
  - a met rate for J's risky tier only, next to the expected count;
  - estimates with intervals in the main text.
- Panel mean: about +1, i.e. roughly 5.5 / 5 / 5.5 / 6.
- No version of Part E alone reaches strong accept. The realistic ceiling, with every part landing, is about 6/6/6/7.

IF PARTS FAIL
- The pre-written contingency mechanics are credited by stats (+0 to +0.5).
- If C and D both fail, the as-designed Table 1 loses rows 5-7. The novelty then rests on rows that are implied or confounded, and mechinterp and sceptic stay at 4.
- With the required changes, the novelty survives on Claim 1's non-implied results (instruction-independence, no second-order lookup, cue-conflict on passages): mechinterp 4.5, others unchanged.
