\section{Setup}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Task and prompt formats.}
We generate belief stories with a port of the released generator of \citet{anonymous2026fitted}: ``Everyone initially sees that the \{$o$\} is in the \{init\}. [\dots] \{$A$\} watches as the \{$o$\} is moved to the \{\textsc{loc}\}. \{$B$\} does not see this happen.'', followed by ``Where does \{$A$\} believe the \{$o$\} is?'' (template in \cref{app:prompts}). Locations are six single-token words. A base story B and a source story S differ only at the \emph{writing token} \{\textsc{loc}\} at position $p$. Two further tasks write the value with one sentence: \emph{paint} (``Later, \{agent\} repainted the \{thing\} \{colour\}.'', six colours) and \emph{schedule} (``Later, \{agent\} moved the \{thing\} to \{day\}.'', seven days). Every prompt is wrapped as a system turn, a user turn, the generation prompt with thinking disabled and an ``Answer:'' prefill, and we read full-vocabulary log-probabilities at the answer position. The formats differ in where, if anywhere, the candidates are named and in the answer instruction (\cref{app:prompts}): \fmtOpt{} (options after the question, ``Answer with exactly one choice'', the format of \citealt{anonymous2026fitted}); \fmtLetter{} (options A--F after the question, ``Answer with one letter'', scored on the letters); and, all with ``Answer with one word'', \fmtPost{} (the neutral sentence ``The room has a box, a basket, \dots{} and a closet.'' after the story), \fmtNone{} (no candidates) and \fmtBefore{} (the options list before the story). A \emph{later mention} is any token after $p$ that names a candidate. The instruction-matched 2$\times$2 of \cref{sec:mentions} adds \fmtListA{} (\fmtOpt{} with the one-word instruction) and \fmtSentB{} (the sentence before the story).

\paragraph{Clamping the key and value channels.}
A later token sees the writing token only through its cached key, which decides how much the token attends to $p$, and its value, which decides what is copied when it does. We call these the \emph{key channel} and the \emph{value channel}. The clamp $\mathcal{C}_K(Z)$ runs the base prompt but sets, at $p$ and in every layer $\ell \ge \ell_0$, the pre-RoPE \texttt{k\_proj} output (all KV groups) to the one cached from run $Z$; $\mathcal{C}_V(Z)$ does the same for \texttt{v\_proj}, and $\mathcal{C}_{KV}(Z)$ does both. Unless stated otherwise $\ell_0 = 0$, and then $\mathcal{C}_{KV}(\mathrm{S})$ reproduces the source run at every later position (\cref{app:impl}).

\paragraph{Measures.}
With $m_{\mathrm{SB}} = \log p(\mathrm{S}) - \log p(\mathrm{B})$ at the answer position, the effect of clamping channel $C$ is measured against the \emph{self-clamp row} $\mathcal{C}_{KV}(\mathrm{B})$, computed in the same batch:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dc}
d_C = m_{\mathrm{SB}}\big(\mathcal{C}_C(\mathrm{S})\big) - m_{\mathrm{SB}}\big(\mathcal{C}_{KV}(\mathrm{B})\big),
\end{equation}
for $C \in \{K, V, KV\}$. The key share is $s_K = \bar d_K / (\bar d_K + \bar d_V)$, a ratio of means over stories. A foreign key can move $m_{\mathrm{SB}}$ without carrying the source value, for instance by weakening B. To isolate identity we also clamp from a story with a third value X that is absent from the story, and with $\Delta_Y(Z) = \log p(Y \mid \mathcal{C}_K(Z)) - \log p(Y \mid \mathcal{C}_{KV}(\mathrm{B}))$ we define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:idk}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{ID}_K = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[ &\Delta_{\mathrm{S}}(\mathrm{S}) - \Delta_{\mathrm{S}}(\mathrm{X}) \\
 + &\Delta_{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{X}) - \Delta_{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{S}) \big].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Any effect shared by all foreign keys cancels, so $\mathrm{ID}_K$ (in nats) is zero if keys carry no identity of the value; $\mathrm{ID}_V$ is defined in the same way with values. Because nats grow with the model's confidence, we also report the scale-free \emph{identity key share} $s_{\mathrm{ID}} = \overline{\mathrm{ID}}_K/(\overline{\mathrm{ID}}_K + \overline{\mathrm{ID}}_V)$, which, unlike $s_K$, excludes non-specific effects.
The \emph{row-restricted splice} runs attention twice per layer and splices output rows by a mask, so that only a chosen group of later tokens sees the swapped key; it is exact in FP32 to $10^{-4}$ (\cref{app:impl}).

\paragraph{Reader heads and the second hop.}
At Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B, heads are ranked on 60 stories by $a_3$, their attention change from the option rows to $p$ under the key clamp, and tested on 60 others. \emph{HeadSplice} lets only chosen heads, in the option rows, see $K_{\mathrm{S}}$: $R(k)$ is the fraction of the option rows' key effect recovered when only the top $k$ heads see it, $\mathrm{KO}(k)$ the fraction lost when only they do not, and $k^*$ is 5\% of heads. \emph{HopSplice} keeps $K_{\mathrm{S}}$ at $p$ but gives only the answer row the option rows' base keys and values; $r_{\mathrm{ans}}$ is the fraction of the key-clamp effect this removes. Both are exact in FP32 (\cref{app:impl}).

\paragraph{The intervention of Anonymous (2026).}
\citet{anonymous2026fitted} patch Mistral-Small-24B and Qwen2.5-72B at the output of (1-based) decoder block 4 over every token of the event span (from ``\{$A$\} watches as'' to the end of the story). For a rank-16 basis $U$ with orthonormal rows, each span position becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:patch}
h \leftarrow h_{\mathrm{B}} + U^{\top} U\,(h_{\mathrm{S}} - h_{\mathrm{B}}).
\end{equation}
In the learned remap M, $U$ is a distributed alignment search basis \citep{geiger2024finding} fit with a six-choice cross-entropy objective so that the patched model ranks $\mathrm{T} = \pi(\mathrm{S})$ first, where $\pi$ is a fixed pair-swap of the six locations. In the control P, $U$ is an unfitted PCA basis of source-minus-base differences, which transfers S. We reuse the released bases (three seeds each; the Qwen2.5-72B PCA bases are reconstructions, labelled so by \citet{anonymous2026fitted}). Following their design, a \emph{key-only exchange} replaces the writing token's key in every block from (1-based) block 6 on by the key of the other patched run, while queries, values and residual states evolve freely; we add the symmetric \emph{value-only exchange}. Unlike our clamp, these exchanges are not exact, because the patch also changes the other span tokens. With $m_{\mathrm{TS}} = \log p(\mathrm{T}) - \log p(\mathrm{S})$, writing $\mathrm{P}{+}K_{\mathrm{M}}$ for P with M's keys and $m(\cdot)$ for $m_{\mathrm{TS}}$ in a run,
\begin{align}
\varphi &= \frac{m(\mathrm{M}) - m(\mathrm{P})}{m(\mathrm{T}) - m(\mathrm{S})}, \label{eq:phi}\\
\psi_K &= \frac{m(\mathrm{P}{+}K_{\mathrm{M}}) - m(\mathrm{P})}{m(\mathrm{M}) - m(\mathrm{P})}, \label{eq:psik}\\
\psi_V &= \frac{m(\mathrm{P}{+}V_{\mathrm{M}}) - m(\mathrm{P})}{m(\mathrm{M}) - m(\mathrm{P})}. \label{eq:psiv}
\end{align}
$\varphi$ is the shift of M beyond P towards T, as a fraction of the natural S$\to$T shift; $\psi_K$ and $\psi_V$ are the fractions of the learned-minus-PCA difference that M's keys or values add to P. All are ratios of means over the 96 of their 120 native story cores in which B, S and T are distinct, after averaging the three fits within each core.

\paragraph{The exchange on another lab's intervention.}
We also apply an exact exchange to the state-token patch of \citet{prakash2025lookbacks} at Qwen2.5-14B, on their template-2 stories, raw prompt and seed-10 pool (\PkN{} pairs). BIND, their patch, sets the residuals at the two state tokens (and the tokens after them), at the output of block $\ell^*$, to those of a counterfactual in which the two binding sentences are exchanged. ID, an identity edit at the same positions, substitutes a drink S absent from the story for the queried state. With $m = \log p(\text{target}) - \log p(\text{queried state})$, $\Phi$ is the patch's effect on $m$, and $\psi_K$ ($\psi_V$) the fraction of it that the clean run reproduces with the patched run's keys (values) at the patched positions from block $\ell^*{+}1$ on; $\kappa = \psi_K/(\psi_K+\psi_V)$. $\ell^*$ is the earliest block of maximal interchange accuracy (IIA) under \fmtNone{}. The formats are \fmtNone{}, \textsc{qnames} (the question names four candidates) and \fmtOpt{} (details in \cref{app:stage6}).

\paragraph{Models, statistics and preregistration.}
We study \nModels{} instruction-tuned models from four families: Qwen2.5-Instruct \citep{yang2024qwen25} at 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B, 32B and 72B, Qwen3-8B, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Mistral-Small-24B-Instruct-2501 \citep{mistral2025small} and OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct \citep{olmo2025two}, in BF16 on GPU. We use $n = 150$ stories per format and model. The row-restricted splice uses $n = 60$ at 7--14B; at Qwen2.5-1.5B it ran, like preregistration A, on CPU in FP32 with $n = 40$. Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from 10{,}000 bootstrap resamples of stories (or pairs), with ratios recomputed within each resample. Eight preregistrations (A--H) were each committed to version control before their run, with the scoring script committed before its outputs were inspected; H was finalised before any output of G was inspected. \Cref{app:prereg} gives every prediction with its recorded outcome and classifies every main claim as confirmatory, exploratory or post hoc.
