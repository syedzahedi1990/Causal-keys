A token that states a value in context, such as \emph{shelf} in ``the candle is moved to the shelf'', can reach a later answer in two ways: a later token can match its cached key and look the value up, or the answer can copy its cached value. Clamping this writing token's key or value separately, we find that later mentions decide the route. In stories, when the candidates are named again after the token, its key carries the value's identity (most of it after a list, part after a neutral sentence, none after a sentence naming no candidate); without a later mention, or with the mention before the token, the identity is copied. On fresh stories in seven model families (7--14B), scored on the forms the models emit, the route decides generated answers: clamping only the key changes them when the candidates are listed after the story, and clamping only the value does the reverse. On counterfactual reading-comprehension passages, an options list added after the passage likewise makes every evaluable model read the answer entity through its keys, and without it through its values; a sentence naming candidates after the passage opens only a small key read. In two 7B models, heads enriched for duplicate-token heads write at each later mention, besides the word itself, a shared component (a flag) that names no candidate; moved to an absent option's row, it moves the answer there and is read by key, but it is neither specific nor the only carrier. A preregistered law that edits of the written value keep the model's own key-to-value ratio failed, even for near-natural steering vectors; a remap trained at the writing token is read far less through its key than that ratio predicts.
