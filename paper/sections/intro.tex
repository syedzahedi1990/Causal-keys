\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

  (a)~In ``\dots\ moved to the shelf'', the writing token \emph{shelf} (blue) leaves a key $K$ and a value $V$ in the cache at every layer. Mechanistic explanations are often stated at the level of components, such as neurons, attention heads or subspaces \citep{vig2020causal,wang2022ioi,geiger2024finding}; for in-context computation, they say that a value written at one token is read through a particular attention channel, or that an intervention's effect is carried by a particular component. A later token sees a context token only through its cached key, which a later query must match, and its value, which is copied when the token is attended to. A written value can thus be \emph{looked up} (\cref{fig:schematic}a): a later token whose content matches the key attends to it, so the key itself carries which value was written. Or it can be \emph{copied}: the reader attends for other reasons, and the identity travels in the value. Lookbacks retrieve a state token through bound ordering IDs, in our terms a copy read \citep{prakash2025lookbacks}, and binding information can sit in query/key subspaces or, in Llama models, mainly in keys \citep{oh2026rebinding}, but what decides which read a prompt uses is not clear.

In the belief stories of \citet{anonymous2026fitted}, one token, the \emph{writing token} (their ``critical token''), states where an object was moved. We clamp its cached key and value separately to those of a run in which a different value was written; clamping both from the first layer reproduces that run exactly (\cref{sec:method}). We vary whether and where the candidate values are mentioned (\cref{fig:schematic}b), in \nModels{} instruction-tuned models (1.5B--72B, four families) on the belief task, and in five 7--14B models on two further tasks.

\textbf{Later mentions decide} (\cref{sec:read}). Whenever the candidates are mentioned again after the writing token, by an option list, lettered options or, from 7B up, a neutral sentence, the value's identity is looked up through the key; without a later mention it is mainly copied through the value. The sentence effect was not predicted; a preregistered, instruction-matched $2{\times}2$ design in four 7--14B models then confirmed it and showed that position is causal: the same list or sentence placed before the writing token opens no key read (\cref{tab:twobytwo}). In the 7--14B models we localised, the re-mentioned words are the main readers: under \fmtOpt{} the option words alone recover \locScaleOptMin{}--\locScaleOptMax{} of the key effect at 7--14B, and under \fmtPost{} the sentence's candidate words recover most of it (\cref{fig:localisation}). The read is an identity lookup in the style of duplicate-token attention \citep{wang2022ioi}, not a read of belief role (\cref{sec:role}).

\textbf{Where an intervention appears to act} (\cref{sec:intervention}). \citet{anonymous2026fitted} fit a rank-16 distributed alignment search (DAS) intervention \citep{geiger2024finding} that remaps locations. In a format that lists the options after the question, exchanging only the writing token's keys between it and an unfitted PCA control transferred much of their behavioural difference, which they presented as a component-level explanation of the tested effects. Re-running their released interventions at 24B and 72B, we find that the behavioural effect is nearly the same in every format, while the channel that appears to carry it flips (\cref{fig:frames}): keys with lettered options, values with no later mention. Our reading, formed after these data and then confirmed for its value half by a preregistered test, is that the edit is present in both channels, and which one appears to carry it depends on which one the format's readers use. An exploratory cross-check supports it: across five formats and two models, the share of the intervention carried by keys tracks the share of the value's identity that the unpatched model reads through keys (\cref{fig:frames}c). A component-level explanation of this intervention is thus a property of the intervention and the prompt together.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~Exact, separate key and value clamps at the writing token, an identity measure, and a row-restricted splice that lets only chosen later tokens see a swapped key (\cref{sec:method}). (ii)~Evidence that later mentions decide whether a value is looked up or copied, that their position is causal, and that the re-mentioned words read the value's identity, not its belief role (\cref{sec:read}). (iii)~A crossover: one learned intervention appears to be carried by keys or by values depending on the format, while its behavioural effect is nearly unchanged (\cref{sec:intervention}). (iv)~Five preregistrations, with every main claim classified as confirmatory, exploratory or post hoc (\cref{app:prereg}); a preregistered transfer law failed at Mistral-Small-24B and held only marginally at Qwen2.5-72B, and a key-share threshold was met in one of three large models (\cref{sec:failed}).
