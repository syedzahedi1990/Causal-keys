\paragraph{Natural formats.}
Over all \nModels{} models (\cref{tab:natural}), the formats fall into three regimes.

\emph{Candidates listed after the writing token.} Under \fmtOpt{} and \fmtLetter{}, $\mathrm{ID}_K$ is large and positive in every model, from \idKOptQwenOnefive{} nats at Qwen2.5-1.5B to \idKOptQwenFourteen{} at Qwen2.5-14B under \fmtOpt{} (A1 at 1.5B on fresh stories and B1 in five held-out models from 3B to 8B, both met; exploratory at 14B and 24--72B).

\emph{No later mention.} Under \fmtNone{}, $\mathrm{ID}_K$ is at most \noneMax{} nats, less than a tenth of its \fmtOpt{} value in every model (paired difference positive in 5/5 held-out models; B2, met), and the identity is mainly copied: $\mathrm{ID}_V$ exceeds $\mathrm{ID}_K$ more than tenfold (exploratory; \cref{tab:decomposition}). \fmtBefore{} names the same candidates before the writing token, which the causal mask keeps them from attending to; its $\mathrm{ID}_K$ is never reliably positive, and negative in nine of ten models (C4's 0.5-nat bound met; sign unexplained).

\emph{A neutral sentence after the story.} \fmtPost{} carries no positive key identity at Qwen2.5-1.5B and 3B, but an intermediate amount in every model from 7B up (\idKPostOlmoSeven{}--\idKPostQwenFourteen{} nats), which we did not predict. The scale-free identity key share $s_{\mathrm{ID}}$ above 1.5B is \idShareOptMinNoOnefive{}--\idShareOptMax{} under \fmtOpt{} and at most \idSharePostMax{} under \fmtPost{} and \idShareNoneMax{} under \fmtNone{} (exploratory; \cref{tab:idshare}).

\paragraph{An instruction-matched $2{\times}2$.}
Preregistration~E crosses \{list, sentence\} with \{before, after\} under one instruction (\cref{tab:twobytwo}); all parts of E1 were met. $\mathrm{ID}_K$ is \twoAfterMin{}--\twoAfterMax{} nats under \fmtListA{} and \twoPostMin{}--\twoPostMax{} under \fmtPost{}, every CI before the story contains zero or lies below it, and both paired differences exclude zero in 4/4 models. Position, not the instruction or the list format, decides whether the key channel opens.

\subsection{Across tasks, families and scale}\label{sec:general}

On the paint and schedule tasks, in five 7--14B models, the paired \fmtOpt{}$-$\fmtNone{} difference in $\mathrm{ID}_K$ is positive in 5/5 models per task (D1, met), and \fmtBefore{} stays within the preregistered 0.5-nat bound in 10/10 cells (D2, met; \cref{tab:tasks}). On IOI \citep{wang2022ioi}, plain sentences carry almost no key identity (G18, met), but the preregistered test that a later list opens a key read was not met (G19; Mistral-7B failed the competence gate; \cref{app:stage5}). Within Qwen2.5, the \fmtOpt{} key share $s_K$ rises from \shareQwenOnefive{} at 1.5B to \shareQwenFourteen{} at 14B and falls to \shareQwenSeventytwo{} at 72B (B3, exploratory; \cref{fig:scale}). The fall is not an artefact of the software or GPU setup (E3, met), but its cause is unknown. Other families give \shareOlmoSeven{}--\shareMistralSeven{} (deeper clamp onsets: \cref{tab:onset}).

Letting only the option words see the source key recovers \locScaleOptMin{}--\locScaleOptMax{} of the full key effect at 7--14B under \fmtOpt{}, and every other group at most \locScaleOptOtherMax{}; under \fmtPost{} the sentence's candidate words recover \locScalePostMin{}--\locScalePostMax{} (D3, met; \cref{fig:readers}a). Conversely, forbidding the six candidate words to attend to the writing token removes the key identity ($r_K$, the fraction left, is $\GfiveRKMin$ to \GfiveRKMax{} in 9/9 format--model cells; G5, met), and cutting a matched control column does not (G6, met). The words must name the candidates at stake: listing B with two other candidates instead of S and X gives no read (G9, met). In lists two named candidates suffice (G10, met for lists); in sentences the read grows with the number named (G10, not met for sentences), and with only S and X named the words carry \GtwelveStwoQwenFourteen{}--\GtwelveStwoQwenSeven{} of the splice effect, most of it not identity-specific (G12, not met; \cref{app:stage5}). The candidate words are thus the main readers in lists and in sentences naming at least three candidates.

\paragraph{What a re-mention must be.}\label{sec:forms}
Varying the re-mentions of \fmtPost{} in four 7--14B models shows a read of the word form, largely up to case ($r_K$: $\mathrm{ID}_K$ relative to an exact repeat). Capitalised and upper-case forms read nearly fully (\GfourteenCaseMin{}--\GfourteenCaseMax{}; G14, which predicted at most 0.75, not met), but a modified noun phrase such as ``wooden shelf'' reads only partly (\GthirteenModifMin{}--\GthirteenModifMax{}; G13, not met). Synonyms and mixed-language translations carry little on our English-scored measure (at most \GfifteenRKMax{}; G15, met). No preregistered token or concept outcome holds (G16, not met). Where the read is lost, the value identity rises (G17, met), but no more than after a sentence naming other words (exploratory; \cref{app:g-forms}).

\subsection{Reader heads and the second hop}\label{sec:heads}

Preregistration H looked inside the option rows at Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B under \fmtOpt{} (\cref{tab:heads}); H1--H5 were met in both. When only the top 5\% of heads by $a_3$ (\HkstarQwenSeven{} and \HkstarMistralSeven{}) see the source key, they recover $R(k^*) = \HoneRQwenSeven{}$ and \HoneRMistralSeven{} of the option rows' read (H1), and blinding them removes \HtwoKOQwenSeven{} and \HtwoKOMistralSeven{} (H2); random sets of equal size recover \HtwoRrandQwenSeven{} and \HtwoRrandMistralSeven{} and remove \HtwoKOrandQwenSeven{} and \HtwoKOrandMistralSeven{} (three-draw means; \cref{fig:readers}b). Ranking by single-head effect does not find the set, nor does leave-one-out at Qwen2.5-7B, and no layer alone recovers more than \HlayerMaxOptQwenSeven{} (exploratory; \cref{app:heads}): the read is joint across layers.

Mean-ablating them at the option rows cuts $\mathrm{ID}_K$ to $\rho_K = \HthreeRhoQwenSeven{}$ and \HthreeRhoMistralSeven{} of its value (control sets $\geq \HthreeRhoRandTwoQwenSeven{}$), raises $\mathrm{ID}_V$ by \HthreeDVQwenSeven{} and \HthreeDVMistralSeven{} nats, and leaves the base answer in \HthreeBaseQwenSeven{} and \HthreeBaseMistralSeven{} of stories (H3). The lookup thus suppresses a copy route that stays available. Cutting the candidate words' edges instead (G5) also restores the value identity in 3/3 models but keeps the answer only at Qwen2.5-14B (G7, not met: a preregistered partial takeover); we do not reconcile the two. We do not claim the lookup replaces the copy: its preregistered tests failed (G11; on IOI, G20).

\paragraph{The second hop.}
The answer need not attend to the writing token (forbidding it leaves $r_K$ at \GeightbRKMistralSeven{}--\GeightbRKQwenSeven{}; G8b, met at 7--14B); it reads the option words. With the source key clamped, giving only the answer row the option rows' base keys and values removes $r_{\mathrm{ans}} = \HfiveAnsQwenSeven{}$ and \HfiveAnsMistralSeven{} of the key-clamp effect, against \HfiveOtherQwenSeven{} and \HfiveOtherMistralSeven{} for all other later rows (H5, met; \cref{fig:readers}c). Unscored (H5), the answer reads them by key: $r_{\mathrm{ans}}(K) = \HfiveAnsKQwenSeven{}$ and \HfiveAnsKMistralSeven{}, against $r_{\mathrm{ans}}(V) = \HfiveAnsVQwenSeven{}$ and \HfiveAnsVMistralSeven{}. The \fmtPost{} line is not evaluable (\cref{app:stage6}). Without a later mention, the same cut leaves \GeightaRatioQwenSeven{}--\GeightaRatioMistralSeven{} of $\mathrm{ID}_V$ (G8a, met).

\subsection{What the duplicate-token account explains}\label{sec:role}

The first hop, a re-mention attending to the writing token, is the pattern of duplicate-token heads \citep{wang2022ioi}. Under \fmtPost{}, the re-mentioned word attends to the writing token far more than the other candidates do, and this attention follows the clamped key one for one, at 7B and 14B ($E$, the excess at the top three heads, = \GoneEQwenSeven{}, \GoneEQwenFourteen{}; $F/E$, its share following the clamp, = \GoneFEQwenSeven{}, \GoneFEQwenFourteen{}; G1) and equally at 1.5B and 3B ($E$ = \GtwoEQwenOnefive{}, \GtwoEQwenThree{}; $F/E$ = \GtwoFEQwenOnefive{}, \GtwoFEQwenThree{}; G2, G3), whose \fmtPost{} key identity is about zero (within the preregistered $\pm 1$-nat margin). All three were met: the match is present without the read. The core of the causally selected readers is of this kind (H4, met): over the top \HfourKCQwenSeven{} and \HfourKCMistralSeven{} heads by $a_3$, which carry 80\% of the read, the median duplicate-token score $D$ (on random repeated sequences) is \HfourDQwenSeven{} and \HfourDMistralSeven{} (whole-model medians \HfourDwholeQwenSeven{} and \HfourDwholeMistralSeven{}, recomputed), the median induction score \citep{olsson2022context} \HfourIQwenSeven{} and \HfourIMistralSeven{}. Only this core is canonical: over the full 5\% set the median $D$ is \HfourDkstarQwenSeven{} and \HfourDkstarMistralSeven{} (recomputed).

We therefore claim no new head type. What we add is that generic duplicate-token heads write an identity flag into the option words' keys, which the answer then reads, mainly by key (H5, secondary); and that the scale dependence enters downstream of hop-1 attention (G2). The channels carry which value was written, not its belief role (E2, met; \cref{tab:role}).
