\section{Later mentions decide whether a value is looked up or copied}\label{sec:claim1}

\textbf{When the candidates are named again after the writing token, later tokens read its key and the value's identity is looked up; without such a mention it is copied from the value.} The position of the mention decides, not the instruction, and the decision reaches generated answers.

\subsection{Fresh stories in seven families}\label{sec:fresh}

In the discovery sample, a list after the story opened a large key read, no later mention left the identity to the value, and a neutral sentence opened a read we had not predicted; an instruction-matched $2{\times}2$ in four of these models showed that where the list or sentence stands decides, not the instruction ($\mathrm{ID}_K$ \twoAfterMin--\twoAfterMax{} nats with the list after the story; every interval before it contains zero or lies below it; \cref{app:discovery}).

The confirmation ran on the 150 fresh stories in those four models and in Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, Phi-4 and Falcon3-7B (\cref{fig:schematic}b). In four model families never examined before, a list of the candidates after the story makes the writing token's key carry most of the value's identity ($s_{\mathrm{ID}} \geq 0.5$ in \JbOneNfMet{} of \JbOneNfEval{}), as in the four discovery-sample models ($s_{\mathrm{ID}}$ \JbRangeSidAfterEightMin--\JbRangeSidAfterEightMax{} over all eight). Without a later mention, the identity is copied through the value in every family ($s_{\mathrm{ID}} \leq 0.1$ under \fmtNone{}). The same list or sentence before the story opens no key read in any of the eight models; in all four new families a list before even lowers the key read below its no-mention level (risky; held), which we cannot explain.

A neutral sentence naming the candidates after the story opens an intermediate key read ($r$ = \JbRangeRPostNfourMin--\JbRangeRPostNfourMax{} of the list read) in \JbThreeNfMet{} of \JbThreeNfEval{} new families (risky; held), as in the four discovery-sample models; it appears with each of the eight sentences and both lexicons (exploratory). A sentence of the same length naming no candidate opens no key read, so the read needs the re-mention (risky in the new families; held). In Llama-3.1-8B, one of the four new families, the null sentence missed the preregistered threshold only because the model shows a small key read without any mention ($r$ = \JbRNoneLlamaEight{}), large as a ratio because its list read is the smallest of the eight; the null sentence adds nothing to it (post hoc reading; \cref{app:fresh}). The test in a 2B model (Gemma-2-2B) was not evaluable: the model failed the competence gate.

\paragraph{Generated answers.}
Clamping only the writing token's key changes the generated answer to the clamped value in \pctn{\JbRangeBetaKAfterEightMin}--\pct{\JbRangeBetaKAfterEightMax} of stories when the candidates are listed after it and in at most \pct{\JbRangeBetaKNoneEightMax} when they are not; clamping only its value does the reverse (\cref{fig:schematic}c). Here `not' means no mention, a list before or a sentence before, and the reverse is a value-only change in \pctn{\JbRangeBetaVNoneEightMin}--\pct{\JbRangeBetaVNoneEightMax} of stories without a mention. The sentence read changes generated answers in seven of the eight models (flip rate \pctn{\JbFivebFlipPostMetMin}--\pct{\JbFivebFlipPostMetMax} after the sentence, \pct{\JbRangeFlipPreEightMax} before it; risky; held in the four new families); at OLMo-2-7B it is visible in log-probabilities but not in generated answers (flip rate 1.0\,\% after, 0\,\% before; risky; did not hold). % verbatim: outcome J part B
The preregistration had no text for this split; the wording is a disclosed decision (\cref{app:decisions}).

\paragraph{The measurement.}
Scored on the forms the models actually emit (coverage $\geq 0.8$ in every cell), every re-scored verdict on the original stories is unchanged (risky; held). $s_{\mathrm{ID}}$ moves by at most 0.05, while the sentence read relative to the list read, $r$, rises under emitted forms by $+$\JbSixbRPostMistralSeven{} at Mistral-7B and by $+$\JbSixbRPostQwenFourteen{} at Qwen2.5-14B, where equivalence within 0.05 is not shown (risky; did not hold): the lower-case score of the discovery sample understated the sentence read. The emitted-form choice agrees with the generated answer in at least \JbSevenAMin{} of items in every cell, and the released remap's effect and channel shares (\cref{sec:claim3}) are unchanged under it (both risky; held; \cref{app:measure}).

\subsection{Natural passages}\label{sec:natural}

On natural reading-comprehension passages with multi-token answers, a later options list makes the model read the passage entity through its cached keys ($s_{\mathrm{ID}}$ \JaSidOptaMistralSeven--\JaSidOptaQwenSeven{} in Gemma-2-9B, Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B), and without it through its values ($s_{\mathrm{ID}}$ at most \JaSidNomLlamaEight{} in Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B and Qwen2.5-7B; \cref{fig:natural}a). This holds for answers of three or more tokens (risky; held) and, with lettered options, in all four models. Llama-3.1-8B with options after missed a preregistered generation-agreement gate by one item, and Mistral-7B failed the free-form competence gate by answering in full sentences (\cref{app:natural}). Options before the passage leave only a small negative key read. On natural passages a related-articles sentence opens only a small key read ($s_{\mathrm{ID}}$ gain \JaFiveDiffQwenSeven--\JaFiveDiffGemmaNine{} in Gemma and Qwen, the evaluable models; risky; did not hold), so on passages our claim concerns a later options list.

\paragraph{Behaviour on passages is a boundary.}
In cue conflict, key and value come from two different counterfactual entities. With lettered options the generated answer follows the key's entity in all four models (key-source rate \JaKeySrcLetaMistralSeven--\JaKeySrcLetaGemmaNine; risky; held). With options answered by name the key wins in two of the three evaluable models (Gemma, Qwen) and not in Mistral (risky; did not hold), although a key that matches no option leaves the value to be copied (risky; held); in free form the decision token follows the value while the continuation breaks (hybrids, an observation), so too few whole answers name the value's entity (risky; did not hold; \cref{fig:natural}b). The channel attributions are therefore not shown to decide behaviour on natural text. Two predictions on 2-bit quantization of the cached values and one on the rate of hybrid answers did not hold either, and the head tests on passages and three further passage tests were not evaluable (\cref{app:natural}).

\subsection{Scope}\label{sec:scope}

On two further templated tasks the same contrast holds in five 7--14B models each; the channels carry which value was written, not its belief role (each at most \roleFracMax{} of a role swap's effect); with the clamp started at about a third of the depth the key read persists in most models (all discovery sample; the last exploratory; \cref{app:discovery}).
