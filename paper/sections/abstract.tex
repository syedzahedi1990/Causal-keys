A token that states a value, such as \emph{shelf} in ``the candle is moved to the shelf'', can reach a later answer in two ways: a later token holding a candidate can match its cached key and look the value up, or the answer can copy its cached value. Clamping this writing token's key and value separately, we find that later mentions decide the route: when the candidates are named again after the token, its key carries which word was written; otherwise the identity is copied. In seven model families the route decides generated answers. Where the candidates are named also decides when the token can still change the answer. In three 7--8B models, overwriting the token's cached key and value from a given layer up changes free-form answers until \pctn{\NqLcFracNoneMin}--\pct{\NqLcFracNoneMax} of the depth, but answers with the candidates listed after the story only until \pctn{\NqLcFracListedMin}--\pct{\NqLcFracListedMax}, a model-specific depth; later overwrites leave most listed answers at the old one, confidently, and overwriting only the layers below changes most of them and no free-form one. Single-site steering largely follows this window; steering at every position, the answer included, escapes it with a named list; and inside the window, the delivered share of the natural key displacement predicts whether an edit reaches a listed answer. In two 7B models the matching heads write, at the later mention, a component that names no candidate, is read by key and suffices to move the answer, but is neither specific nor the only carrier. A preregistered law that edits keep the model's own key-to-value ratio failed.
