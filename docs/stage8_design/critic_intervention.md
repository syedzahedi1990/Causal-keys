DESIGN AS WRITTEN, IF THE PREDICTIONS COME OUT:
- Mechinterp: 4 -> 4. Contribution stays at 2. The result would read as "steering vectors that write the same low-dimensional lexical code are read like the natural edit", which is the corollary of Section 3 this reviewer already named. They asked for a non-identity edit at a depth where the key route is open, and the design does not provide one: JC6 is an identity edit at a depth where the key route is closed.
- Skeptic: 4 -> at most 4.5. Their first request (an identity edit at a depth chosen on held-out data) is met. Their second (a non-identity edit at an open depth) is not. The "close to definitional" objection survives, because the counted edits are about 0.95-cosine aligned with the natural displacement, and JC2 and JC3 are close to guaranteed.
- Generalist: 4 -> 4.5-5. They asked for exactly this: a third, independent intervention with a preregistered matched-depth prediction. They are less likely to notice that the share statistic is compressed. But if the JC6 sentence ("the boundary is the kind of edit, not its depth") is printed as written, someone on the panel can refute it from Table H: BIND IIA is 0 at blocks 0-23 and s_ID(OPTIONS, 24) is 0.04.
- Stats: 5 -> 5, perhaps 5.5 for the attempt. This reviewer criticised F4's r over five points that span 0-1 "by construction" and alternatives that are "easy to reject". JC3's Pearson r >= 0.85, its share MAD and the JC2 crossover have the same defect. The headline could also be carried by E1 and E2, whose result is implied by the pilot.
- Panel mean about 4.5-4.6, roughly +0.25 to +0.5 over today: no clear accept.

DESIGN AS WRITTEN, IF IT FAILS:
- Section 4 retreats to "near-natural edits only", and that confirms the panel's reading.
- Mechinterp and skeptic: -0 to -0.5 on contribution.
- Generalist: about 4.
- Stats: about 5 (they credit honest failure).
