\textbf{The lookup has two hops: heads whose core is canonical duplicate-token heads match the writing token's key at each later mention and write a flag into that mention's row, which the answer then reads by key.} The flag suffices to move the answer but is neither specific nor the only carrier, and the question sets the sign of the read.

\paragraph{Two hops.}
In the discovery sample, at Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B, 5\,\% of the heads, the \emph{reader heads}, read the writing token's key at the option words (hop~1): when only they see the clamped key, they recover \HoneRQwenSeven{} and \HoneRMistralSeven{} of the option rows' read, random sets almost none. Their core is canonical duplicate-token heads \citep{wang2022ioi}, though the full set is not. The answer then reads the option words, not the writing token (hop~2), mainly by key (unscored there; tested below). We claim no new head type (\cref{app:heads}).

\paragraph{What the readers write.}
The reader heads write one vector per layer into the re-mentioned word's row. We call it the \emph{flag} (fit on the 60 stories on which the heads were ranked, tested on 100 held-out ones). Injected with no key clamp into the row of an option absent from the story, a version fit without that word moves the answer there (\JdOneRatioQwenSeven{} / \JdOneRatioMistralSeven{} of the key read's identity effect; the answer changes in \pct{\JdOnePiXQwenSeven} / \pct{\JdOnePiXMistralSeven} of stories) (risky; held; \cref{fig:flag}a). It names no candidate: fit with or without the value's identity, it is the same direction with the same effect. But specificity against structured controls failed: at Qwen2.5-7B the mean reader output moves the answer \JdTwoRatioMeanHQwenSeven{} as much, and at Mistral-7B the story's own write with its flag component removed, norm-matched, \JdTwoRatioOrthMistralSeven{} as much (risky; did not hold; the mean output contains the flag by construction, which explains part of this; \cref{app:flag}).

\paragraph{Read by key, not carried by one direction.}
The answer reads the injected flag by key, as it reads the natural one (giving only the answer row the option rows' original keys removes \JdFourKQwenSeven{} / \JdFourKMistralSeven{} of its effect, their original values \JdFourVQwenSeven{} / \JdFourVMistralSeven{}). One direction per layer does not carry the key read ($\rho_K$ = \JdThreeRhoFlagQwenSeven{} / \JdThreeRhoFlagMistralSeven{} of it is left when the direction is removed): other carriers exist; the flag is sufficient but not necessary (risky; did not hold; \cref{fig:flag}b). The prediction failed on its controls: removing the flag direction leaves about half of the key read in both models, and at Qwen the control directions, which overlap the flag, remove 31\,\% and 44\,\%. % verbatim: outcome J part D
Ablating the whole reader heads removes far more of the read (discovery sample).

\paragraph{The sign of the read.}
Asked which option was \emph{not} mentioned rather than which was, both reads turn negative ($\mathrm{ID}_K$ $\JdSevenQoutQwenSeven$ and $\JdSevenQoutMistralSeven$ nats), and the same frozen reader heads carry the reads under both questions: the sign follows the task (\cref{fig:flag}c). A negative use of duplicate detection is known \citep{wang2022ioi,mcdougall2023copy}; we do not present the polarity as a discovery. In indirect-object identification (IOI) with the names re-mentioned in a parenthesis, the channels separate: the key read is negative, the value read positive ($\mathrm{ID}_K$ $\JdSignIoiIDKQwenSeven$ against $\mathrm{ID}_V$ \JdSignIoiIDVQwenSeven{} nats at Qwen2.5-7B; the same signs at Mistral-7B). The negative IOI read is applied at the answer's key read of the listed names (restoring the listed names' keys and values for the answer row alone removes \JdRouteKVQwenSeven{} / \JdRouteKVMistralSeven{} of it, their keys alone \JdRouteKQwenSeven{} / \JdRouteKMistralSeven{}, their values alone \JdRouteVQwenSeven{} / \JdRouteVMistralSeven{}; risky; held). But a different (or a distributed) reader applies the negative read: the ten answer-side heads ranked as the flag's top readers in lists carry neither negative read (risky; did not hold; the scorer's reading: a different reader), though even in lists they carry only part of it. Whether the belief reader heads also carry the IOI read was met at Qwen2.5-7B and is not evaluable at Mistral-7B (a transfer gate missed).

\paragraph{Scale, binding and a non-candidate key.}
The flag is written in sentences as in lists at 1.5B, 3B and 7B. Whether it carries the size of the sentence read came out mixed, the preregistered outcome: the test failed at 1.5B and 7B (3B not evaluable), and at 1.5B the injected flag was read by key, not by value as predicted (both risky; did not hold; \cref{fig:flag}d). Post hoc, the preregistered natural ratio contains a gain that every non-candidate receives; on a contrast specific to the absent option, the injected flag reproduces the natural ratio in sign and size (\cref{app:scale}). Two further risky predictions did not hold: the initial-state flag does not carry binding in a way separable from order, and a key that names no candidate does not act as removing the base value's flag (\cref{app:flag}).
