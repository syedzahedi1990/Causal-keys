\textbf{Even steering vectors close to the natural edit depart from the natural channel ratio; the format law describes the model's own written value and does not predict how an edit is attributed.} Which cache channel an intervention appears to act through depends on the prompt (discovery sample; exploratory here) and on the edit. % verbatim (first sentence): C-12 outcome (e), entry J part C
The motivating case is the released remap of \citet{anonymous2026fitted}, a rank-16 distributed alignment search subspace \citep{geiger2024finding} over the move-event span at block 4, credited under \fmtOpt{} partly to the writing token's keys (\cref{app:remap}): at Qwen2.5-72B its effect barely changes with the format, yet its keys carry \psiLetterQwen{} of it with lettered options and \psiNoneQwen{} without a later mention, its values the reverse (discovery sample; exploratory at this model, preregistered for a refit at 24B).

\paragraph{The law and its test.}
The preregistered law extends the format law to edits: an edit written at the writing token at depth $l$ is read through key and value in the model's own ratio at that depth and format. With $\psi_C$ the edit's identity effect through channel $C$ as a share of the natural write's, from block $l{+}1$ on, and $\lambda = \log(\psi_K/\psi_V)$,\label{eq:lambda} the law holds in a cell when the 90\,\% interval of $\lambda$ lies inside $\pm\log 1.25$; writing the source run's residual there is our equivalence control (\cref{app:law}). We tested the edit families of \cref{fig:law} and \emph{non-lexical} steering vectors in up to three models, at blocks 3 to 15, in four formats (\cref{app:edits}).

\paragraph{Steering vectors.}
In the preregistered core test the steering vectors' departures are small but graded ($\bar\lambda$ = $\JcOnePoolEoneMistralSeven$ to $\JcOnePoolEtwoMistralSeven$ at Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B): the format law does not transfer to edits as a channel-ratio law, and the prediction did not hold (\cref{fig:law}a). Every pooled $\bar\lambda$ and its interval lie inside the margin but below zero; the 90\,\% intervals of \JcOneCoreNe{} of the \JcOneCoreN{} single statistics, all at block \JcOneCoreNeDepthMin{} or deeper, cross the margin, though none lies wholly outside it; at Llama-3.1-8B the vectors from unrelated sentences failed too, one statistic wholly outside. At the shallowest depth every statistic is equivalent ($|\lambda| \leq \JcOneLamThreeAbsMax$; exploratory); pooled over depths, in the three models the keys under-read the steering vectors by 4.5--12\,\% ($\bar\lambda$ $\JcTwoPoolEoneLlamaEight$ to $\JcTwoPoolEtwoLlamaEight$). % verbatim: outcome J part C
The failing statistics sit in cells with a small natural key read (\JcOneNeIDKMin--\JcOneNeIDKMax{} nats), and from block 7 on the under-read appears also where the key read is large (post hoc; \cref{app:lawresults}). The vectors' key share still follows the natural one across formats (exploratory; \cref{fig:law}b).

\paragraph{Edits far from the natural write.}
Edits that change the value through a learned remap are read mainly through the value ($\bar\lambda$ = $\JcFourPoolEfourQwenSeven$ / $\JcFourPoolEfourMistralSeven$): the key channel does not read them as it reads the written word, so the prediction\rsk{} that the remap keeps the model's ratio did not hold. The pooled values rest on statistics near the floor of $\psi$ at block 7; at block 3 alone $\bar\lambda$ is $\JcFourDepthThreeQwenSeven$ and $\JcFourDepthThreeMistralSeven$ (post hoc). Its key share still follows the format, below the natural one (exploratory; \cref{app:lawresults}). Unlike the released remap, it writes only the writing token and was fit without a mention (one remap, two seeds, two models). The autoencoder test was not evaluable: its alignment gate measured reconstruction at positions the dictionary's published fit excludes; under either gate tried after the data it would not have held (\cref{app:edits}). The predictions\rsk{} that identity-carrying components off the natural write or the lexical span keep the ratio, and that non-lexical vectors mark the token-form boundary, did not hold: the components are read mainly through the value (\cref{fig:law}c), and the non-lexical vectors also write far from the natural state, so the boundary was not isolated; we claim neither that the token-form account holds for edits nor that it is contradicted. The reader heads read the steering vectors like the natural value, but that prediction failed through a random-set control the natural value also fails (post hoc reading), and was not evaluable for the autoencoder and remap (\cref{fig:law}d).

\paragraph{Post hoc: distance from the natural write.}
After the data we related the departures to $\nu$, the relative distance of an edit's cached keys and values from the natural ones ($\nu = 0$ for the natural write): of the $\lambda$ statistics of identity-carrying edits, \JcNuEqLow{} of \JcNuNLow{} with $\nu < 0.1$ are equivalent, \JcNuEqMid{} of \JcNuNMid{} with $\nu$ between 0.1 and 0.3, and none of the \JcNuNHigh{} beyond, \JcNuOutHigh{} of which have intervals wholly outside the margin (Spearman $\JcNuRhoNu$ [$\JcNuRhoNuLo$, $\JcNuRhoNuHi$]; \cref{app:nu}). The bands were chosen after the data, $\nu$ is collinear with the key cosine, and the pattern is largely between families. $\nu$ is a diagnostic to report, not an explanation (distances at the intervention site can miss unnatural pathways; \citealp{liu2026wrong}). A hypothesis for a future test ties this to \cref{sec:claim2}: if the key route selects through a thresholded match (half a flag barely moves the answer) while the value read decays slowly with distance, an edit far from the natural write loses its key read first.

\paragraph{The binding swap of \citet{prakash2025lookbacks}.}
At block 28 of Qwen2.5-14B an identity edit is copy-read like the natural value ($\kappa \leq 0.30$), while the binding swap there is key-read; key-flat reading is not a property of depth for identity edits, though this does not separate kind from depth, because the natural read there is itself copy-only and the swap changes no answer in two of the three formats (\cref{app:binding-j}). Binding forms only after the identity key route closes in every model screened, so a binding swap cannot be compared with an identity edit at a depth where the key route is open\rsk; the four earlier predictions on this swap, which failed, stand, and a window prediction was not evaluable.
