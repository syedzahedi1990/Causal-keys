Mechanistic explanations are often stated at the level of components, such as attention heads or subspaces \citep{vig2020causal,wang2022ioi,geiger2024finding}, for instance that a value written in context is read through a particular attention channel, or that an intervention's effect is carried by a particular component. A later token sees a context token only through its cached key, which a later query must match, and its value, which is copied when the token is attended to. A written value can thus be \emph{looked up} (\cref{fig:schematic}a): a later token whose content matches the key attends to it, so the key itself carries which value was written. Or it can be \emph{copied}: the reader attends for other reasons, and the identity travels in the value. Lookbacks bind a state token through ordering IDs \citep{prakash2025lookbacks}; we find their state-token patch (an address and payload swap, in their terms) reaches the answer mainly through the state tokens' keys, with a value share of \HeightPsiV{} without a later mention, where an identity edit at the same tokens is copied through the value (\cref{sec:prakash}). Binding information can sit in query/key subspaces or, in Llama models, mainly in keys \citep{oh2026rebinding}; what decides which read a prompt uses is unclear.

In the belief stories of \citet{anonymous2026fitted}, one token, the \emph{writing token} (their ``critical token''), states where an object was moved. We clamp its cached key and value separately to those of a run in which a different value was written; clamping both from the first layer reproduces that run exactly (\cref{sec:method}). We vary whether and where the candidate values are mentioned (\cref{fig:schematic}b), in \nModels{} instruction-tuned models (1.5B--72B, four families) on the belief task, and in five 7--14B models on two further templated tasks.

\textbf{Later mentions decide} (\cref{sec:read}). Whenever the candidates are mentioned again after the writing token, by an option list, lettered options or, from 7B up, a neutral sentence, the value's identity is looked up through the key; without a later mention it is mainly copied through the value. The sentence effect was not predicted; a preregistered, instruction-matched $2{\times}2$ design in four 7--14B models then confirmed it and showed that position is causal: the same list or sentence placed before the writing token opens no key read (\cref{tab:twobytwo}). The read has two hops (\cref{sec:heads}): at Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B, 5\,\% of attention heads read the writing token's key at the option words (if only they see the swapped key, \HoneRQwenSeven{} and \HoneRMistralSeven{} of its effect there is recovered), and the answer position then reads the option words directly (\cref{fig:readers}). The re-mentioned words are the main readers in lists and in sentences naming at least three candidates (\cref{sec:readers}). Hop~1 is duplicate-token attention \citep{wang2022ioi}; canonical duplicate-token heads form the readers' core (\cref{sec:role}). This is not new, and with the causal mask it predicts the format dependence; but it is present also at 1.5B and 3B, whose key identity is about zero, so the scale dependence arises downstream.

\textbf{Where an intervention appears to act} (\cref{sec:intervention}). \citet{anonymous2026fitted} fit a rank-16 distributed alignment search (DAS) remap of locations \citep{geiger2024finding} and, with options listed after the question, traced much of its difference from an unfitted PCA control to the writing token's keys, their component-level explanation of the tested effects. Re-running their released interventions at 24B and 72B, we find the behavioural effect nearly the same in every format, while the channel that appears to carry it flips (\cref{fig:frames}): keys with lettered options, values with no later mention. Our reading, formed after these data, is that the edit is present in both channels and the format's readers decide which one appears to carry it. Two preregistered tests then supported it: values carry the edit where keys do not read the identity, and a remap refit without later mentions shows the same crossover (\cref{sec:refit}). On the state-token binding swap of \citet{prakash2025lookbacks}, which we replicate at Qwen2.5-14B, our preregistered extension failed (\cref{sec:prakash}): the swap is key-dominant in each evaluable format, so the law predicting an edit's key share from the natural read fails (preregistration~H, H7), and later mentions shift its key share by $+\HnineDiff{}$, below the preregistered 0.4 (H9). A component-level explanation of an intervention is thus a property of the intervention, of what it changes, and of the prompt in which it is read. \Cref{tab:accounts} (\cref{app:discussion}) sets four accounts against these observations. Each, ours included, is contradicted somewhere: our account's preregistered extension to the binding swap failed, and the lookback account fits that swap, post hoc, only in part.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~Exact key and value clamps, row-, head- and answer-row-restricted splices, and an exact key/value exchange on another lab's patch. (ii)~Later mentions decide look-up versus copy; their position is causal (A--E). (iii)~The mechanism at 7B: sparse duplicate-token readers, then the answer's direct read of the option words, mainly through their keys (an unscored contrast); the duplicate-token match is not sufficient across scale (G, H1--H5). (iv)~An identity edit's attributed channel flips with the read format (C, E4, F; H11 at block~0); on the binding swap the preregistered law fails and later mentions shift the key share by only $+\HnineDiff{}$ (H7--H10), at another depth. (v)~Eight preregistrations (A--H); every failed prediction is reported (\cref{app:prereg,sec:failed}).
