\textbf{In two 7B models (Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B), heads enriched for duplicate-token heads match the writing token's key at each later mention and write a flag into that mention's row, which the answer reads by key.} The flag suffices to move the answer but is neither specific nor the only carrier, and the question sets the sign of the read.

\paragraph{Two hops.}
In the discovery sample, 5\,\% of heads, the \emph{reader heads}, read the writing token's key at the option words (hop~1): seeing the clamped key alone, they recover \HoneRQwenSeven{} and \HoneRMistralSeven{} of the option rows' read, random sets almost none. They are enriched for duplicate-token heads \citep{wang2022ioi}: the \HfourKCQwenSeven{} and \HfourKCMistralSeven{} heads carrying 80\,\% of the read have median duplicate-token scores of \HfourDQwenSeven{} and \HfourDMistralSeven{}, against \HfourDwholeQwenSeven{} and \HfourDwholeMistralSeven{} over all heads (exploratory). Mean-ablating them at the option rows leaves \HthreeRhoQwenSeven{} and \HthreeRhoMistralSeven{} of the key read and raises the value read: a copy route stays available (\cref{app:heads}). The answer reads the option words, not the writing token (hop~2), mainly by key.

\paragraph{What the readers write.}
The reader heads write one vector per layer into the re-mentioned word's row. We call it the \emph{flag} (fit on the 60 ranking stories, tested on 100 others). Injected with no key clamp into the row of an option absent from the story, a version fit without that word moves the answer there (\JdOneRatioQwenSeven{} / \JdOneRatioMistralSeven{} of the key read's identity effect; the answer changes in \pct{\JdOnePiXQwenSeven} / \pct{\JdOnePiXMistralSeven} of stories)\rsk. Like the natural key clamp, the injection adds the flag at that row and removes it at the base value's row; added alone, one flag moves the answer \JdDoseOneQwenSeven{} / \JdDoseOneMistralSeven{} as much as the natural clamp, half a flag almost not at all (exploratory; \cref{fig:flag}a). Its mean direction names no candidate: fit with or without the value's identity, it is the same direction with the same effect, although each story's write also carries its word (\cref{app:flag}). The answer reads it by key, as it reads the natural one (giving only the answer row the option rows' original keys removes \JdFourKQwenSeven{} / \JdFourKMistralSeven{} of its effect, their original values \JdFourVQwenSeven{} / \JdFourVMistralSeven{}). But the prediction\rsk{} that it is specific failed against structured controls: at Qwen2.5-7B the mean reader output moves the answer \JdTwoRatioMeanHQwenSeven{} as much as the flag, and at Mistral-7B the story's own write with its flag component removed, norm-matched, \JdTwoRatioOrthMistralSeven{} as much (the mean output contains the flag by construction; \cref{app:flag}). And one direction per layer does not carry the key read ($\rho_K$ = \JdThreeRhoFlagQwenSeven{} / \JdThreeRhoFlagMistralSeven{}): other carriers exist; the flag is sufficient but not necessary (\cref{fig:flag}b). That prediction\rsk{} failed on its controls: removing the flag direction leaves about half of the key read in both models, and at Qwen the control directions, which overlap the flag, remove 31\,\% and 44\,\%; ablating the whole heads removes far more (above). % verbatim: outcome J part D

\paragraph{The sign of the read.}
Asked which option was \emph{not} mentioned, both reads turn negative ($\mathrm{ID}_K$ $\fpeval{round(\JdSevenQoutQwenSeven,1)}$ and $\fpeval{round(\JdSevenQoutMistralSeven,1)}$ nats), and the same frozen reader heads carry the reads under both questions (\cref{fig:flag}c); the flag fit with the belief question, injected under this one, reproduces its negative effect (\JdNineQoutRatioQwenSeven{} and \JdNineQoutRatioMistralSeven{} of it; exploratory), so the sign is applied after the first hop. In indirect-object identification (IOI) with the names re-mentioned in a parenthesis, the key read is negative and the value read positive ($\mathrm{ID}_K$ $\fpeval{round(\JdSignIoiIDKQwenSeven,2)}$ against $\mathrm{ID}_V$ \fpeval{round(\JdSignIoiIDVQwenSeven,2)} nats at Qwen2.5-7B; the same signs at Mistral-7B). The negative IOI read is applied at the answer's key read of the listed names (restoring their keys and values for the answer row alone removes \JdRouteKVQwenSeven{} / \JdRouteKVMistralSeven{} of it, their keys alone \JdRouteKQwenSeven{} / \JdRouteKMistralSeven{}, their values alone \JdRouteVQwenSeven{} / \JdRouteVMistralSeven{})\rsk. The prediction\rsk{} that the ten answer-side heads ranked as the flag's top readers in lists apply it failed: a different (or a distributed) reader applies the negative read (the scorer's ``different reader''), though even in lists these ten carry only part of hop~2. That the belief readers carry the IOI read\rsk{} was met at Qwen2.5-7B and is not evaluable at Mistral-7B (a gate missed). The flag is written in sentences as in lists at 1.5B--7B, but four further predictions\rsk{} did not hold: that it carries the size of the sentence read across scale and is read by value at 1.5B (the preregistered outcome: ``Mixed.''; \cref{app:scale}), that the initial-state flag carries binding separably from order, and that a key naming no candidate acts as removing the base value's flag.
