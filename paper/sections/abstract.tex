A token that states a value in context, such as \emph{shelf} in ``the candle is moved to the shelf'', can be read by later tokens through its attention key, matched by a later query, or its value, which is copied. We clamp the two channels separately at this writing token in \nModels{} models from four families and, in a subset of them, on two further tasks. Whenever the candidates are mentioned after the writing token, by an option list or, in larger models, a neutral sentence, the value's identity is looked up through the key, and in the models we localised the re-mentioned words are the main readers; otherwise the value is mainly copied, even when the same list precedes the writing token. A learned intervention from prior work, whose effect was traced in part to the writing token's keys in a format that lists the options after the question, has nearly the same behavioural effect in every format. Yet at Qwen2.5-72B its keys alone reproduce \psiLetterQwen{} of its difference from an unfitted control with lettered options, and its values \psiVNoneQwen{} with no later mention; across formats, the share its keys carry tracks how much the unpatched model reads the value through keys. Which component appears to carry this intervention thus depends on the prompt; we separate preregistered from exploratory results and report the predictions that failed.
