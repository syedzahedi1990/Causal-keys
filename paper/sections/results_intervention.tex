\section{Where an intervention appears to act}\label{sec:intervention}


\citet{anonymous2026fitted} read their key exchange between M and P as causal evidence that keys induced by the learned patch contribute to the behavioural difference between the two, under their tested multilayer intervention and the prompt format we call \fmtOpt{}. With their released bases and encoder, at pinned model revisions, our runs reproduce their saved answers under \fmtOpt{} and pass the preregistered gate (\cref{sec:method}). We then change only the format and add a value-only exchange. Two comparisons are confirmatory and met in both models: C3, $\psi_K(\fmtNone{}) < 0.5\,\psi_K(\fmtOpt{})$, and E4, $\psi_V(\fmtNone{}) \geq 0.5$ and $> \psi_K(\fmtNone{})$. The preregistered predictions on $\varphi$, C1 and C2, are tested in \cref{sec:failed}. The rest is exploratory; \cref{tab:frames} gives every number with its interval.

\paragraph{Behaviour barely changes across formats; the carrying channel flips.}
$\varphi$ lies between \phiOptMistral{} and \phiLetterMistral{} at Mistral-Small-24B and between \phiNoneQwen{} and \phiBeforeQwen{} at Qwen2.5-72B, nearly flat across formats (the small \fmtOpt{}$-$\fmtNone{} difference at Qwen2.5-72B is reliable; \cref{sec:failed}), and the PCA control transfers the source location in every format. Yet at Qwen2.5-72B, M's keys reproduce \psiLetterQwen{} of the M$-$P difference under \fmtLetter{} and \psiOptQwen{} under \fmtOpt{}, but only \psiPostQwen{} under \fmtPost{}, \psiNoneQwen{} under \fmtNone{} and $\psiBeforeQwen$ under \fmtBefore{}, while M's values do the reverse ($\psi_V$ = \psiVLetterQwen{}, \psiVOptQwen{}, \psiVPostQwen{}, \psiVNoneQwen{}, \psiVBeforeQwen{}). Mistral-Small-24B (\cref{fig:frames}) and the removal fractions $\rho_K$ and $\rho_V$ (\cref{tab:frames}) show the same crossover. This matches the natural factorial (\cref{sec:mentions}): a strong key read with options after the writing token, a partial one with the neutral sentence, and little or none without a later mention.

\paragraph{A cross-check against the natural read.}
If the readers decide which channel carries the edit, the remap's key share $\psi_K/(\psi_K+\psi_V)$ in a format should track the identity key share $s_{\mathrm{ID}}$ of the unpatched model in that format (\cref{sec:mentions}), which we measured on our own stories in the same two models at the same pinned revisions. Across the ten format--model cells the two agree closely (\cref{fig:frames}c; Pearson $r = \crossR{}$). The largest gap is under \fmtPost{}, where the remap's key share (\chSharePostMistral{} at Mistral-Small-24B, \chSharePostQwen{} at Qwen2.5-72B) is below the natural one (\natSharePostMistral{}, \natSharePostQwen{}). This comparison was made after all data were collected, and ten cells from two models are few, so we treat it as exploratory.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
In \fmtLetter{}, \fmtOpt{}, \fmtPost{} and \fmtNone{} the prompt is identical up to the end of the story, so under the causal mask M writes the same key and the same value at the writing token in all four; only their readers differ. The edit is therefore present in both channels, and which one appears to carry it depends on which channel the format's readers use. This reading was formed after the data of preregistration C; its value half was then preregistered (E4) and confirmed. We localised the readers only at 1.5--14B (\cref{sec:readers}); that the later mentions are also the readers at 24B and 72B is an inference. The two addition fractions $\psi_K$ and $\psi_V$ sum to less than one in every cell, while the removal fractions $\rho_K$ and $\rho_V$ sum to more than one in most cells (\cref{tab:frames}). The two channels therefore do not combine additively, and part of the edit may also travel through the other patched span tokens, which we did not exchange. In their format, \fmtOpt{}, we reproduce their key result: M's keys alone reproduce a substantial part of the M$-$P difference in both models ($\psi_K$ = \psiOptMistral{} and \psiOptQwen{}) and, in a comparison they did not make, more of it than M's values do. They scoped their claim to their tested setting; here that setting must include the prompt, because which channel carries the intervention is a property of the intervention together with the prompt it is measured under. We did not re-test their colour-lookup questions, and we score log-odds fractions where they scored argmax agreement.

\subsection{Preregistered predictions that failed}\label{sec:failed}

We had preregistered a transfer law (\cref{app:prereg}): without a later mention the remap can act only through values, so it should transfer only as far as the value channel carries it. C1 predicted $\varphi(\fmtNone{})$ within $\pm 0.15$ of $\kappa_V$, a value-channel completeness we estimated beforehand by clamping, from data released with \citet{anonymous2026fitted} (0.53 at Mistral-Small-24B, 0.73 at Qwen2.5-72B). C2 predicted $\varphi(\fmtOpt{}) - \varphi(\fmtNone{}) > 0$. C1 fails at Mistral-Small-24B ($\varphi = \phiNoneMistral{}$ [\phiLoNoneMistral{}, \phiHiNoneMistral{}]) and is met only at the edge of the margin at Qwen2.5-72B (\phiNoneQwen{} [\phiLoNoneQwen{}, \phiHiNoneQwen{}]). C2 fails at Mistral-Small-24B ($-0.003$ [$-0.019$, $+0.013$]) and is met, but small, at Qwen2.5-72B ($+0.034$ [$+0.018$, $+0.049$]). The remap transfers about as fully without later mentions as with them, and $\kappa_V$ did not reliably predict this transfer. Separately, C4 predicted a key share $s_K \geq 0.80$ under \fmtOpt{} at Mistral-Small-24B, Qwen2.5-32B and Qwen2.5-72B; it holds only at Qwen2.5-32B (\cref{fig:scale}), while its \fmtBefore{} control is met in all three.
