A token that states a value in context, such as \emph{shelf} in ``the candle is moved to the shelf'', can be read by later tokens through its attention key, matched by a later query, or its value, which is copied. We clamp the two channels separately at this writing token in \nModels{} models from four families and, in a subset of them, on two further tasks. Whenever the candidates are mentioned after the writing token, by an option list or, in larger models, a neutral sentence, the value's identity is looked up through the key, and in the models we localised the re-mentioned words are the main readers; otherwise the value is mainly copied, even when the same list precedes the writing token. A learned intervention from prior work, whose effect was traced in part to the writing token's keys in a format that lists the options after the question, has nearly the same behavioural effect in every format. Yet at Qwen2.5-72B its keys alone reproduce \psiLetterQwen{} of its difference from an unfitted control with lettered options, and its values \psiVNoneQwen{} with no later mention; across formats, the share its keys carry tracks how much the unpatched model reads the value through keys. In a preregistered test at Mistral-Small-24B, a refit of the intervention with no candidate named after the story shows the same crossover: the format in which it is read, more than the one in which it was fit, decides which component appears to carry it. We separate preregistered from exploratory results and report the predictions that failed.
