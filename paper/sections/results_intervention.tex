\textbf{Even steering vectors close to the natural edit depart from the natural channel ratio; the format law describes the model's own written value and does not predict how an edit is attributed.} % verbatim: C-12 outcome (e), entry J part C
Qualitatively, the format dependence transfers to near-natural edits, whose key share follows the natural one across formats (exploratory; \cref{fig:law}b); quantitatively, the channel ratio does not. Where an intervention appears to act depends on the prompt (discovery sample; exploratory here) and on the edit, so it has to be measured.
The motivating case is the released remap of \citet{anonymous2026fitted}, a rank-16 distributed alignment search subspace \citep{geiger2024finding} over the move-event span at block 4 (\cref{app:remap}): at Qwen2.5-72B its effect barely changes with the format, yet its keys carry \psiLetterQwen{} of it with lettered options and \psiNoneQwen{} without a later mention, its values the reverse (discovery sample; preregistered without a mention, exploratory with letters).

\paragraph{The law and its test.}
The preregistered law extends the format law to edits: an edit written at the writing token at depth $l$ is read through key and value in the model's own ratio there. With $\psi_C$ the edit's identity effect through channel $C$ as a share of the natural write's, and $\lambda = \log(\psi_K/\psi_V)$,\label{eq:lambda} the law holds in a cell when the 90\,\% interval of $\lambda$ lies inside $\pm\log 1.25$ (\cref{app:law}). We tested the edit families of \cref{fig:law} and \emph{non-lexical} steering vectors in up to three models, at blocks 3 to 15, in four formats (\cref{app:edits}).

\paragraph{Steering vectors.}
In the preregistered core test the steering vectors' departures are small but graded ($\bar\lambda$ = $\JcOnePoolEoneMistralSeven$ to $\JcOnePoolEtwoMistralSeven$ at Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B): the format law does not transfer to edits as a channel-ratio law, and the prediction did not hold (\cref{fig:law}a). Every pooled $\bar\lambda$ and its interval lie inside the margin but below zero; the intervals of \JcOneCoreNe{} of the \JcOneCoreN{} single statistics, all at block \JcOneCoreNeDepthMin{} or deeper, cross the margin, none wholly outside it, so each is an equivalence not shown rather than a departure shown. The preregistered power analysis expected the test to accept a key-to-value ratio of about 0.9 and to reject 0.8; the pooled ratios are \fpeval{round(exp(\JcOnePoolEtwoMistralSeven),2)}--\fpeval{round(exp(\JcOnePoolEoneMistralSeven),2)} (post hoc comparison). At Llama-3.1-8B the vectors from unrelated sentences failed too, one statistic wholly outside. At the shallowest depth every statistic is equivalent (exploratory); pooled over depths, in the three models the keys under-read the steering vectors by 4.5--12\,\%. The failing statistics sit in cells with a small natural key read, and at block 7 the under-read appears also where the key read is large, inside the margin (post hoc; \cref{app:lawresults}). % verbatim: "4.5--12 %", outcome J part C; "about 0.9" and "0.8", entry J part C (Power)

\paragraph{Edits far from the natural write.}
Edits that change the value through a learned remap are read mainly through the value ($\bar\lambda$ = $\JcFourPoolEfourQwenSeven$ / $\JcFourPoolEfourMistralSeven$): the key channel does not read them as it reads the written word, so the prediction\rsk{} that the remap keeps the model's ratio did not hold. The shortfall is relative to the model's own ratio and grows with depth: at block 3 the remap's key share with the options after is \JcKappaEfouraOptThreeQwenSeven{} and \JcKappaEfourbOptThreeQwenSeven{} (two seeds, Qwen2.5-7B) against a natural \JcSigmaOptThreeQwenSeven{} (exploratory); at block 7 after a sentence $\psi_K$ is \JcFourPsiKWpostSevenQwenSeven{} / \JcFourPsiKWpostSevenMistralSeven{} against $\psi_V$ \JcFourPsiVWpostSevenQwenSeven{} / \JcFourPsiVWpostSevenMistralSeven{} (post hoc). Fitting without a later mention does not by itself explain this: at Mistral-Small-24B a remap so fit by the released recipe was key-read with letters (preregistration F, \cref{app:refit}; post hoc comparison). The autoencoder test\rsk{} was not evaluable: its alignment gate failed, probably because it measured reconstruction at positions the dictionary's published fit excludes (\cref{app:edits}). The predictions\rsk{} that identity-carrying components off the natural write or the lexical span keep the ratio, and that non-lexical vectors mark the token-form boundary, did not hold: the components are read mainly through the value, and the non-lexical vectors also write far from the natural state (post hoc), so the boundary was not isolated, and we claim neither that the token-form account holds for edits nor that it is contradicted (\cref{app:lawresults}). The prediction that the reader heads read the steering vectors failed through a random-set control the natural value also fails (post hoc reading), and was not evaluable\rsk{} for the autoencoder and remap (\cref{fig:law}d). Post hoc, the departures follow $\nu$, the relative distance of an edit's cached keys and values from the natural ones: of the $\lambda$ statistics of identity-carrying edits (E3's included), \JcNuEqLow{} of \JcNuNLow{} with $\nu < 0.1$ are equivalent, \JcNuEqMid{} of \JcNuNMid{} with $\nu$ between 0.1 and 0.3, and none of the \JcNuNHigh{} beyond (\cref{app:nu}). The bands were chosen after the data, $\nu$ is collinear with the key cosine, so the pattern is partly mechanical, and $\nu$ is largely confounded with the kind of edit (beyond 0.3, \JcNuOtherAbove{} of the \JcNuNHigh{} statistics are from the remap, non-lexical vectors or components); it is a diagnostic, not an explanation \citep{liu2026wrong}. A hypothesis for a future test: if the key route selects competitively among flagged rows (half a flag barely moves the answer while the base value's row keeps its own; \cref{sec:claim2}), a far edit loses its key read first.

\paragraph{The binding swap of \citet{prakash2025lookbacks}.}
At block 28 of Qwen2.5-14B an identity edit is copy-read like the natural value ($\kappa \leq 0.30$), while the binding swap there is key-read; key-flat reading is not a property of depth for identity edits, though this does not separate kind from depth: the natural read there is itself copy-only, and the swap changes no answer in two of the three formats. Binding forms only after the identity key route closes in every model screened (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B), so a binding swap cannot be compared with an identity edit at a depth where the key route is open\rsk; the four earlier predictions on this swap, which failed, stand, and a window prediction\rsk{} was not evaluable (\cref{app:binding-j}).
